LFGD local 3D-hit reconstruction concept
========================================

Purpose
-------
Develop an LFGD-specific replacement for the initial 3D-hit construction,
while keeping the existing HFGD reconstruction downstream of that step.
Unlike the SFGD, light in the LFGD is shared across several fibres and is
correlated along columns, so one virtual cube must not automatically become
one independent 3D hit.

Proposed reconstruction sequence
--------------------------------

1. Find a track-like cluster directly from the fibre hits, using DBSCAN or a
   local straight-line fit. No external or pre-existing track direction is
   assumed at this stage.

2. Estimate the local main direction of that cluster and select the dominant
   detector axis. Use the axis whose absolute direction component is largest,
   with a nominal 45-degree criterion. Directions near the boundary require a
   fallback or ambiguity treatment.

3. Divide the cluster into slices perpendicular to the dominant axis. Each
   slice has the width of one fibre pitch (currently 10 mm).

4. In each slice, initially assume that the deposited charge is concentrated
   at one subvoxel in the plane through the centre of the slice. The coordinate
   along the dominant axis is assigned to the slice centre. Its intrinsic
   uncertainty is approximately pitch/sqrt(12), or 2.9 mm for a 10 mm pitch.

5. Use the two fibre views perpendicular to the dominant track axis to
   reconstruct the two transverse coordinates and the slice charge. The fibre
   view parallel to the dominant axis is not used for this local position fit;
   it may later help with charge sharing or ambiguity resolution.

   Example for an x-dominant track:

     - z-oriented fibres constrain y;
     - y-oriented fibres constrain z;
     - x-oriented fibres are approximately parallel to the track and are not
       used for the local transverse position.

6. Implement and compare two reconstruction methods:

   a. Centre-of-gravity method

      Calculate each transverse coordinate from the charge-weighted fibre
      positions in the corresponding view. Estimate the charge separately in
      both views after correcting for their collection efficiencies, then
      combine the two estimates. Do not simply sum both views, because that
      would count the same deposited charge twice.

   b. Slice-restricted light-map likelihood

      Test the 10 x 10 subvoxel hypotheses in the central plane of the slice.
      For hypothesis i and fibre f, predict

          mu_f = Q_c * L_fi,

      where L_fi is the light-map fraction/probability and Q_c is the produced
      slice charge. The sum of measured fibre charges provides the initial
      charge estimate. For a fixed subvoxel, Q_c can be profiled (for a Poisson
      model) as

          Q_c_hat(i) = sum_f(q_f) / sum_f(L_fi).

      Select the subvoxel and charge giving the best likelihood or Poisson
      deviance.

Initial spatial scope: central slice only (plus/minus zero)
-----------------------------------------------------------

Start with only fibres belonging to the slice being reconstructed (plus/minus
zero neighbouring slices). The L_fi values are simply the fractions or
probabilities for this restricted response.

Do not initially include fibres from the neighbouring slices (plus/minus one).
Their signals can contain light produced by charge deposited in those slices,
so including them without a simultaneous multi-slice model could bias the
central-slice position or charge. A plus/minus-one extension should only be
tested later with the charges of adjacent slices fitted simultaneously, or
with another explicit treatment of their contributions.

Validation and stored output
----------------------------

Store both the centre-of-gravity and light-map estimates, including position,
charge, fit quality/likelihood, uncertainty, selected views, dominant axis,
slice index, and source cluster identifier. This permits direct validation
before either estimate is passed to the standard downstream tracking.

The first study should test the point-like central-plane hypothesis for tracks
up to about 30 degrees from an axis and investigate performance toward 45
degrees. At larger angles, the charge distribution within one slice can be too
extended for a single-subvoxel template; a short-segment template would then
be the natural refinement.

Multiple tracks and delta electrons
-----------------------------------

Multiple-track charge sharing is deferred. Once local candidates and track
continuity exist, two nearby tracks can be represented by a sum of two
light-map templates. The fibre view parallel to the dominant axis may then be
used to constrain how charge is shared. Forks from delta electrons should be
identified as local branches rather than forcing all associated fibre hits
into the main-track candidate.
